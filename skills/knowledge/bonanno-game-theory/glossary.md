# Glossary

**Assessment** — A strategy profile plus beliefs at information sets, used in dynamic games with imperfect information (Ch 10–12).
**Backward induction** — Solving a finite perfect-information game from terminal decision nodes back to the root (Ch 2).
**Bayesian updating** — Updating probabilities by conditioning on observed positive-probability evidence (Ch 8, Ch 10, Ch 12).
**Behavioral strategy** — A probability distribution over actions at each information set in an extensive-form game (Ch 6).
**Belief revision** — Changing beliefs after unexpected or zero-probability evidence; used for off-path reasoning (Ch 8, Ch 12).
**Cardinal payoff** — A payoff number meaningful for expected-utility calculations, unique up to positive affine transformation in EU settings (Ch 4–6).
**Chance move** — A move by nature with exogenously specified probabilities (Ch 3).
**Common knowledge** — Everyone knows an event, everyone knows that everyone knows it, ad infinitum (Ch 7).
**Common knowledge of rationality** — The infinite hierarchy that all players are rational, know all are rational, etc. (Ch 9).
**Consistent assessment** — An assessment whose beliefs arise as limits from fully mixed perturbations (Ch 11).
**Dominance** — A comparison showing one strategy is never worth using because another strategy performs better under relevant contingencies (Ch 1, Ch 5).
**Expected utility** — Utility of a lottery computed as probability-weighted utility of outcomes (Ch 4).
**Frame** — The structural part of a game: players, choices, outcomes, timing, and information, before preferences/payoffs are added (Ch 1–3).
**Game** — A frame plus preferences or payoffs (Ch 1).
**Harsanyi consistency / like-mindedness** — Coherence of beliefs across players, often grounded in a common prior structure (Ch 8).
**Imperfect information** — A dynamic-game setting where a player cannot distinguish among some decision nodes (Ch 3).
**Incomplete information** — Uncertainty about payoff-relevant features, available actions, or strategic environment (Ch 13–15).
**Information set** — Nodes among which a player cannot distinguish when choosing (Ch 3).
**Lottery** — A probability distribution over outcomes (Ch 4).
**Mixed strategy** — A probability distribution over pure strategies (Ch 5).
**Nash equilibrium** — A strategy profile where each player is best-responding to the others (Ch 1, Ch 5).
**Ordinal payoff/preference** — A ranking of outcomes where only order matters, not payoff differences (Ch 1–3).
**Perfect Bayesian equilibrium** — An assessment satisfying sequential rationality and Bayesian/belief-revision consistency requirements (Ch 12).
**Rationalizability** — Survival of strategies compatible with rational choice and beliefs about others' rationality (Ch 5, Ch 9).
**Sequential equilibrium** — A sequentially rational and consistent assessment (Ch 11).
**Sequential rationality** — Optimality at every information set given beliefs and continuation strategies (Ch 10–12).
**State-space approach** — Modeling incomplete information directly with states, information, and beliefs (Ch 13–15).
**Strategy** — A complete contingent plan specifying what a player does wherever they might have to act (Ch 2–3).
**Subgame** — A self-contained continuation game starting at a singleton information set and not cutting information sets (Ch 3).
**Subgame-perfect equilibrium** — A strategy profile inducing Nash equilibrium in every subgame (Ch 3, Ch 6).
**Support** — The pure strategies assigned positive probability by a mixed strategy (Ch 5).
**Type** — A representation of a player's private information/payoff facts/beliefs in Harsanyi type-space models (Ch 15).
**Weak sequential equilibrium** — A sequentially rational assessment with Bayesian updating at reached information sets (Ch 10).
