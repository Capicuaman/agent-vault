# Elicitation

**Elicitation** = obtaining accurate information without direct questions. Direct questions activate self-presentation filters; the counterparty edits their answer for image, liability, or commercial interest. Elicitation uses statements, hypotheticals, silence, and oblique references so the counterparty speaks freely — brags, corrects, defends, or fills silence — and reveals the underlying truth.

Rooted in intelligence tradecraft, sales discovery methodology, and clinical interviewing.

## When to use this skill

Load this skill when the user is preparing for, drafting scripts for, or debriefing:

- Interviews with **service providers who work on commission or fees**: real estate brokers, contractors, lawyers, notaries, financial advisors, insurance agents, wedding vendors
- **Medical consults** where the doctor may minimize / overprescribe based on time or incentive
- **Salary and offer negotiations** (either as candidate or employer)
- **Job interviews** — either side; candidates hiding weaknesses, hiring managers hiding team problems
- **Sales discovery calls** — extracting real budget, timeline, decision process, competing solutions
- **Sensitive family conversations** — inheritance, care decisions, financial disclosures
- **Due diligence / vendor selection** — SaaS demos, agency pitches, consultant interviews
- **Journalism-style investigation** — sources who won't answer directly

**Do NOT use elicitation for:**
- Contracts, legal terms, medical dosages, safety-critical specs → direct questions only
- Casual conversation with friends / family with no adversarial dynamic
- Situations requiring informed consent (therapy, coaching, interviews for research)
- Any context where indirection would be manipulative rather than counter-manipulative

## Core principle

> A direct question asks the counterparty to volunteer information against their interest.
> An elicitation prompt asks the counterparty to correct, complete, or defend a statement — which is *with* their self-image interest.

**Example — Direct:**
> "How many houses have you sold this year in beachfront properties?"

Response is filtered for image. Usually inflated or vague.

**Example — Elicitation (bracketing + silence):**
> "I imagine in a good year on beachfront a broker closes 3 or 4 deals max, right? The zone doesn't give more."
> *(silence)*

Now they either brag ("no, I closed 7 this year in these specific projects…") or confirm ("yeah, more or less"). Either answer is unfiltered data.

## The 9 techniques

Detailed examples in [references/techniques.md](references/techniques.md).

| # | Technique | Mechanism | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bracketing** | State an incorrect range | Extracting numbers (price, volume, timeline) |
| 2 | **False statement** | State something wrong on purpose | Extracting market/technical knowledge |
| 3 | **Feigned ignorance** | "I don't quite understand…" | Extracting expertise depth |
| 4 | **Flattery + assumed expertise** | "You've seen so many of these…" | Extracting anecdotes, patterns, opinions |
| 5 | **Silence** | 3–10 seconds after their answer | After any short/evasive answer |
| 6 | **Oblique reference** | "A friend was told that…" | Extracting comparisons, biases |
| 7 | **Quid pro quo** | Share a fact first | Extracting specific numbers |
| 8 | **Hypothetical / third-party framing** | "If you were in our situation…" | Extracting honest recommendation |
| 9 | **Complaint bait** | "Others couldn't answer this…" | Triggering competitive defense |

## Workflow when helping the user

When the user asks to apply elicitation to a specific conversation:

### 1. Understand the target and stakes
- Who is the counterparty? What's their incentive structure?
- What is the user trying to find out that the counterparty would hide, minimize, or inflate?
- What decision does the user need to make based on this?

### 2. Classify information by extraction method
Sort the user's questions into three buckets:
- **Direct-safe** — screening credentials, contract terms, legal/medical facts, logistics. Ask straight.
- **Elicit-first** — pricing, opinions, sesgos, market realities, recommendations, "if you were me". Never ask directly.
- **Mixed** — start elicit, escalate to direct if answer is evasive.

### 3. For each elicit-first item, choose the right technique
Match the technique to the type of information. See [references/techniques.md](references/techniques.md) for the mapping table.

### 4. Structure the conversation script
- **Opening**: set tone (usually mention competition + human context — deactivates commercial mask)
- **Screening**: semi-direct with softeners
- **Market / opinion / hidden info**: elicitation-first
- **Contracts / logistics**: direct
- **Close**: quid pro quo reciprocity + no-commitment exit line

### 5. Include a "how to listen" section
The technique only works if the user knows what to observe:
- What they DIDN'T say
- Specific numbers vs. round generalities
- First-hand anecdotes vs. abstract market talk
- Reaction to silence (fills with data = expert; fills with fluff = improvising)
- What option they push (reveals their commission bias)

See [references/listening-guide.md](references/listening-guide.md) for the full observation checklist.

### 6. Add a cheat-sheet
End every deliverable with 8–12 memorized phrases the user can deploy under pressure. Localize to the user's language and register.

### 7. Provide a post-conversation evaluation grid
Rate the counterparty on:
- Corrected false statements with data (= honest)
- Volunteered specific numbers (= experienced)
- Recognized own structural bias (= high integrity)
- Filled silence with data vs. fluff
- Balanced options vs. pushed one

## Deliverable format

When producing an elicitation script, always structure it as:

1. **Operating principle** — one-paragraph reminder of direct vs. elicit
2. **Techniques table** — the 9 techniques localized to context
3. **How to listen** — observation checklist
4. **Conversation blocks** — mixing direct + elicit as appropriate
5. **Cheat sheet** — 8–12 ready-to-use phrases in target language
6. **Post-conversation evaluation** — grid for scoring the counterparty

Keep the tone respectful of the counterparty. Elicitation is *counter-manipulation*, not manipulation — it protects the user from filtered answers, not tricks the counterparty into revealing secrets they morally shouldn't.

## Ethical guardrails

- Never use to extract information the counterparty is legally / ethically obligated to keep private
- Never use against people with less power / information (children, patients under care, subordinates in performance conversations)
- Never combine with actual deception about material facts (identity, intent, stakes)
- The user should always be prepared to answer, if asked, "why are you asking me that?" honestly

If the user's request violates these, flag it and refuse or reshape.
