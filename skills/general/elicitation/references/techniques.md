# The 9 Elicitation Techniques — Deep Reference

Each technique includes: **mechanism**, **when to use**, **template**, **examples in multiple domains**, and **failure modes**.

---

## 1. Bracketing

**Mechanism:** State an incorrect numeric range. The counterparty's expertise instinct forces them to correct you toward the real number.

**When to use:** Extracting any number the counterparty would inflate or hide — price, volume, timeline, salary, occupancy, commission rate.

**Template:**
> "I imagine [subject] is somewhere between [X] and [Y]…"
> "I heard it's usually around [inflated or deflated figure]…"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "I imagine a broker in this zone closes 3 or 4 beachfront deals a year max."
- **Contractor:** "For a bathroom renovation like this we're figuring $8–12k."
- **Salary:** "For this role at your stage, I've seen ranges from $X to $Y…"
- **Doctor:** "I've read recovery for this is usually 4–6 weeks."
- **SaaS demo:** "Companies our size usually pay somewhere between $20k and $40k a year for this."

**Failure mode:** If you bracket too wide, they just confirm without specificity. Keep the range narrow enough that landing outside it feels wrong to them.

---

## 2. False statement

**Mechanism:** State something inaccurate about their domain. Experts cannot resist correcting; novices confirm or dodge.

**When to use:** Extracting market knowledge, technical depth, and detecting whether the counterparty actually knows their domain.

**Template:**
> "I understand that [confident but wrong statement]."
> "We were told that [technical claim]."

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "We understand real beachfront pays about 40% more than 'ocean view'." (Real number varies 20–60% — they'll give the real range for that zone.)
- **Contractor:** "Salt-air corrosion is really just a paint issue, right?" (A real coastal contractor will lecture you for 10 minutes about hardware, rebar, HVAC.)
- **Financial advisor:** "Index funds always beat active management." (Response reveals sophistication.)
- **Job interview (candidate):** "I assume your team ships weekly?" (Response reveals real cadence.)

**Failure mode:** If the false statement is *too* wrong, you look uninformed and lose credibility. Calibrate to "plausibly wrong."

---

## 3. Feigned ignorance

**Mechanism:** Present yourself as not understanding a technical topic. Experts explain with pride; posers minimize.

**When to use:** Extracting depth of expertise; checking whether the counterparty can explain their domain to a layperson (a real proxy for mastery).

**Template:**
> "I don't quite understand how [X] works…"
> "Sorry, this is new to us — can you walk us through [X]?"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "We don't really understand how AMPI and MLS work — can you explain?"
- **Notary:** "I've never understood how the ISR exemption on primary residence actually applies…"
- **Doctor:** "I don't fully get the difference between these two treatments…"
- **VC pitch (as founder):** "We're not sure we understand your fund's stage focus — can you explain your last 3 checks?"

**Failure mode:** Overplayed ignorance signals you can be exploited. Use once or twice per meeting, not as a running posture.

---

## 4. Flattery + assumed expertise

**Mechanism:** Compliment the counterparty's experience and imply they've seen many cases like yours. They fill the frame with anecdotes and patterns.

**When to use:** Extracting opinions, war stories, and patterns the counterparty would otherwise generalize.

**Template:**
> "You've probably seen dozens of situations like ours — what's the pattern?"
> "Someone with your years in [domain] must have a read on this…"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "You've probably seen a lot of families in our exact situation — three siblings, one property. What tends to happen?"
- **Lawyer:** "You've probably drafted hundreds of these agreements — where do most disputes come from?"
- **Contractor:** "You've done a lot of coastal properties — what's the mistake most owners make?"
- **Sales prospect (as seller):** "You've evaluated a lot of tools like ours — what usually decides it?"

**Failure mode:** Empty flattery reads as manipulative. The compliment must be specific to a real fact about them (years, zone, project type).

---

## 5. Silence

**Mechanism:** After the counterparty gives a short or evasive answer, wait 3–10 seconds without speaking. Human discomfort with silence forces them to keep talking, and the second half of their answer is almost always more honest than the first.

**When to use:** Constantly. Especially after: prices, opinions, recommendations, denials, and vague generalities.

**Template:**
No template. Just close your mouth, maintain eye contact, and count.

**How to hold silence:**
- Nod slowly
- Write in your notebook (this gives you an excuse for the pause)
- Say "hmm…" once and stop
- Sip water

**Examples:**
- Broker gives a price. You say nothing. They add: "…of course that assumes we renovate the kitchen."
- Doctor recommends a treatment. You say nothing. They add: "…but honestly, watchful waiting is also reasonable in your case."
- Contractor gives a quote. You say nothing. They add: "…that doesn't include electrical."

**Failure mode:** Silence with a hostile face reads as confrontation. Keep face open, curious.

## 6. Oblique reference

**Mechanism:** Reference a similar situation (a friend, a case, a neighbor) instead of asking about the direct topic. The counterparty responds to the reference and reveals their view on your actual case.

**When to use:** Extracting opinions on emotionally loaded topics; comparisons; sensitive judgments.

**Template:**
> "A friend was in a similar situation and [outcome]. Have you seen that?"
> "We heard about a case where [outcome]. Is that common?"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "We heard about a family with three siblings who rented instead of selling and two years later were fighting over the house. Have you seen that?"
- **Doctor:** "My aunt had a similar surgery and had complications for months. How often does that happen?"
- **Contractor:** "A neighbor did this same renovation and the paint peeled within a year. Is that a common mistake?"
- **Salary:** "A friend at another company at this level was offered $X plus equity. Is that in the ballpark here?"

**Failure mode:** If the reference is obviously invented or too convenient, you lose trust. Have a real reference or don't use it.

---

## 7. Quid pro quo

**Mechanism:** Share a specific fact or number first. Reciprocity norm makes the counterparty match with their own specific fact.

**When to use:** Extracting specific numbers (prices, comps, bids) after generic ones have failed.

**Template:**
> "We calculated [our estimate]. How does that compare to what you see?"
> "We already got a quote of [X] from someone else. What's your read?"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "We think the house is worth around $X million. How do you see it?"
- **Contractor:** "We got a bid of $45k from another contractor for the same scope. Is that in the range?"
- **Salary:** "The other offer we have is $X base plus Y equity. Where does this offer land?"
- **Vendor:** "The competitor's proposal came in at $Z. Where are you?"

**Failure mode:** If your shared number is invented, they'll sense it. Use real data or an honestly-labeled estimate ("we think", "we calculated").

---

## 8. Hypothetical / third-party framing

**Mechanism:** Ask the counterparty to place themselves in your position, or frame the question as "if you had a sister in this situation…" — this deactivates the commercial script and activates human advice mode.

**When to use:** Extracting honest recommendation despite structural bias (broker's commission, salesperson's quota, doctor's specialty).

**Template:**
> "If you were in our exact situation — [full context] — what would you do?"
> "What would you advise your sister / brother / mother if they were us?"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "If you were in our exact situation — mother is ill, ISR exemption is active, three siblings — would you sell or rent?"
- **Doctor:** "If it were your mother, would you do surgery or wait?"
- **Financial advisor:** "If this were your own money at 55 with our savings level, would you shift to bonds?"
- **Contractor:** "If it were your own beach house, would you renovate before selling or sell as-is?"

**Failure mode:** They may deflect with "well, everyone's situation is different…" — press once with "understood, but with our specific facts, what's your gut?" — then stop.

---

## 9. Complaint bait

**Mechanism:** Complain about a shortcoming of others in the counterparty's profession. Competitive instinct makes them differentiate themselves with concrete data.

**When to use:** Screening credibility; getting them to demonstrate capability instead of assert it.

**Template:**
> "Other [professionals] we spoke to couldn't [do X]…"
> "Our biggest frustration has been that no one can [Y]…"

**Examples:**
- **Broker:** "Other brokers gave us prices with no real comparables — just opinions." (A good broker pulls comps up right there.)
- **Contractor:** "The other quotes we got were vague one-liners." (A pro sends you a line-item breakdown.)
- **Doctor:** "The last specialist couldn't explain the trade-offs clearly." (A good one draws the trade-offs on paper.)
- **Vendor:** "Every SaaS demo we've seen this quarter has been a slide deck." (A real one opens the product.)

**Failure mode:** Coming across as chronically dissatisfied. Use once, early, then move on.

---

## Combining techniques

The strongest sequences combine 2–3 techniques:

- **Bracketing + silence**: state the wrong range, then wait.
- **False statement + oblique reference**: "We heard that [wrong]. A friend told us [conflicting]. What's real?"
- **Flattery + hypothetical**: "You've seen so many of these — if you were us, what would you do?"
- **Complaint bait + quid pro quo**: "Others couldn't show us comps. Here's what we found on Zillow — what do you see?"

---

## Universal accelerators

Regardless of technique:

- **Have two people** in the meeting. One talks, one only writes. Rotate every 15 min.
- **Ask permission to record.** Refusal is data.
- **Never sign anything in the first meeting.** State this upfront.
- **Take physical notes.** The visible pen slows the counterparty's pace and gives you an excuse to pause.
- **Debrief within 60 minutes.** Elicitation extracts more than you can consciously process live.
