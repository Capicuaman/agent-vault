# Domain Adaptations

How elicitation shifts across common high-stakes conversations. For each domain: **counterparty bias**, **what to elicit**, **key techniques**, **cheat-sheet phrases**.

---

## Real estate broker (selling)

**Bias:** Commission = fast one-time payout on sale. Will inflate listing price to win exclusivity, then pressure price cuts.

**Elicit:**
- Real transaction volume (not listings)
- Real comparables (sold, not listed)
- True price band vs. aspirational
- Whether they push sell over rent (usually yes)
- Contractor "cousins" (conflict of interest)

**Techniques:** Bracketing (volume), false statement (market claims), quid pro quo (with Zillow/Inmuebles24 data), complaint bait (comps), hypothetical (if this were yours).

**Phrases:**
- "We imagine a broker in this zone closes 3–4 beachfront deals a year max…"
- "A neighbor's house is listed at $X on [portal] — what's your read?"
- "Other brokers couldn't show us actual sold comps."
- "If you were us with our exact tax situation, would you sell or rent?"

## Contractor / renovation bids

**Bias:** Wants to win the bid; will underbid then add change orders. Or overbid on unfamiliar work.

**Elicit:**
- Whether they've done this specific work (not "similar" work)
- Real timeline (not sales timeline)
- Hidden costs (electrical, permits, disposal)
- Whether their crew is direct hire or subbed out

**Techniques:** False statement (technical), feigned ignorance (materials), silence (after quote), quid pro quo (competing bid), complaint bait (vague quotes).

**Phrases:**
- "So salt-air is just a paint issue, right?"
- "The other quote came in at $X — where are you?"
- *(after quote, silence — they'll disclose what's not included)*
- "We don't understand the difference between [option A] and [option B]…"

## Salary negotiation (as candidate)

**Bias:** Recruiter/manager wants to anchor low, hide band ceiling, avoid disclosing others' comp.

**Elicit:**
- Band ceiling for this role
- Sign-on/equity flexibility
- Timeline pressure on their side
- Comparable comps in the org

**Techniques:** Bracketing (state a band that includes their real ceiling), oblique reference (a friend at similar company), quid pro quo (competing offer if real), silence (after first offer).

**Phrases:**
- "For this level at your stage, I've seen bands from $X to $Y — where does this role sit?"
- "A friend at [comparable company] at this level is at $X — is that in the ballpark?"
- *(after their offer, silence)*
- "If we can't close the gap on base, what's flexible on sign-on or equity?"

## Salary negotiation (as employer / hiring)

**Bias:** Candidate wants to anchor high, hide other offer weakness, appear more courted than they are.

**Elicit:**
- Real competing offers (or their absence)
- Actual timeline urgency
- Cultural fit concerns hidden by "excitement"

**Techniques:** Feigned ignorance (about their other process), oblique reference, silence, hypothetical.

**Phrases:**
- "How are the other conversations going? — no need for specifics."
- "If we were the only offer on the table, would timeline change?"
- *(after they name a number, silence)*
- "If money were equal across your options, what would decide it?"

## Doctor visit (major decision)

**Bias:** Time pressure, specialty bias (surgeon recommends surgery), risk-aversion in disclosure, may minimize alternatives.

**Elicit:**
- Alternatives they didn't mention
- What they'd do for a family member
- True prognosis distribution (not just median)
- Whether watchful waiting is a real option

**Techniques:** Hypothetical (family member), oblique reference (similar cases), feigned ignorance (about alternatives), silence.

**Phrases:**
- "If it were your mother in this situation, would you do surgery or wait?"
- "My aunt had a similar diagnosis and chose watchful waiting — how common is that path?"
- "I don't fully understand why we're not considering [alternative treatment]…"
- *(after prognosis, silence)*

**Ethical note:** Don't manipulate a doctor into unsupported treatment. Use elicitation to surface options, not to steer them.

## Sales discovery call (as buyer)

**Bias:** Salesperson qualifies you for their process, hides pricing until late, oversells feature coverage.

**Elicit:**
- Real pricing floor
- Actual product limits (not roadmap promises)
- Which customers churned and why
- Their competition's real strengths

**Techniques:** Bracketing (pricing), false statement (feature claims), complaint bait (other vendors), quid pro quo (naming competitor bids).

**Phrases:**
- "Companies our size usually pay $X–$Y for this — where do you land?"
- "We assume you don't handle [specific case] — right?"
- "The [competitor] demo showed [feature] — how do you compare?"
- "Every vendor claims [X] — what's the honest limitation?"

## Sales discovery call (as seller)

**Bias:** Buyer hides real budget, real timeline, real decision-makers, and shopping around.

**Elicit:**
- True budget range
- Actual decision process and other people involved
- Timeline pressure (or lack of it)
- Competing solutions being evaluated

**Techniques:** Bracketing (budget), oblique reference (similar customers), feigned ignorance (their internal process), hypothetical.

**Phrases:**
- "Customers like you usually budget $X–$Y for this kind of project — is that your range?"
- "Most CEOs we work with have a CFO involved by this stage — how's your side working?"
- "If we were the only option, would you move this quarter or next?"
- "What other approaches are you seriously considering?"

## Legal consult / lawyer

**Bias:** Bills by the hour, incentive to complicate; or fixed-fee incentive to simplify away real risks.

**Elicit:**
- Real risk distribution (not worst case, not best case)
- Where they've seen this go wrong
- Whether the case is routine or novel for them
- Whether junior associates will do the work

**Techniques:** Flattery + assumed expertise, oblique reference, hypothetical, complaint bait.

**Phrases:**
- "You've drafted hundreds of these — where do most disputes actually come from?"
- "A friend had a similar case that dragged for 18 months — how likely is that here?"
- "If this were your family's situation, would you fight or settle?"
- "Other lawyers gave us worst-case scenarios without probabilities — what's your realistic read?"

## Family / inheritance / care decisions

**Bias:** Family members hide financial pressure, resentments, side-agreements. Emotions distort disclosure.

**Elicit:**
- Real financial stress
- Hidden preferences ("I don't care" often means "I care a lot")
- Third-party influence (spouses, in-laws)
- Whether stated preference is genuine

**Techniques:** Oblique reference (other families), hypothetical, silence, quid pro quo (share your own vulnerability first).

**Phrases:**
- "I've been thinking about [my own concern] — what's on your mind?"
- "A friend's family went through this and the sibling who said 'I don't care' turned out to care most. Do you actually have a preference?"
- "If money weren't a factor, what would you want?"
- *(long silence after their answer)*

**Ethical note:** Use with extreme care. Family elicitation should serve mutual understanding, not maneuvering.

## VC / investor pitch (as founder)

**Bias:** VC hides true fund thesis, real check-size range, existing portfolio conflicts, and follow-on probability.

**Elicit:**
- Real check size range at this stage
- Whether they have conflicts in your space
- Follow-on rate
- Real decision timeline

**Techniques:** Bracketing, quid pro quo (naming other funds in the round), oblique reference (other portcos), feigned ignorance (their process).

**Phrases:**
- "At our stage, we've seen checks from $X to $Y — where do you typically land?"
- "[Other fund] is in the round at $X — where would you come in?"
- "We noticed you invested in [adjacent company] — how do you think about overlap?"
- "We don't fully understand your decision process — can you walk us through?"

## Journalism / investigation

**Bias:** Source hides institutional knowledge, protects colleagues, fears retaliation.

**Elicit:**
- Facts they can't state directly but can confirm
- Names / relationships in the network
- Timing of key events

**Techniques:** False statement (deliberately wrong version — they correct), oblique reference, silence, flattery.

**Phrases:**
- "We heard [wrong version of events] — is that right?"
- "Others in your position have described [X]. Does that match?"
- *(silence after any partial answer)*
- "Someone with your years on this beat has probably seen this pattern before…"

**Ethical note:** Journalistic elicitation has established norms — off the record, on background, deep background. Follow them.

---

## Universal cross-domain principles

1. **Match register to context.** Elicitation in an executive interview looks different than with a contractor — but the underlying moves are the same.
2. **Localize to language.** Spanish, English, formal, informal — translate phrases carefully; the psychology holds, the exact words don't.
3. **Never combine with material deception.** Elicitation surfaces truth; lying about who you are or what's at stake corrupts it.
4. **Debrief in writing within 60 minutes.** You extracted more than you noticed.
