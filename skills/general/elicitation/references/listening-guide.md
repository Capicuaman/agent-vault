# How to Listen — The Observation Half of Elicitation

Elicitation only works if you **notice** what the counterparty reveals. Most of the signal is not in the words.

## The two-person rule

Whenever possible, bring a second person. Roles:
- **Speaker** — runs the script, deploys techniques
- **Listener** — writes only, does not interrupt

Rotate every 15 minutes. Compare notes only after the meeting. This doubles your bandwidth and prevents the classic failure mode: getting so busy asking that you don't hear.

If you must go alone, take physical notes (not laptop). The visible pen slows the counterparty and buys you pause time.

## Six signals to observe

### 1. What they DIDN'T say

More diagnostic than what they said. Track topics that came up and got deflected, minimized, or ignored.

Examples:
- Broker talks a lot about marketing, says nothing about screening buyers → weak on buyer qualification
- Contractor discusses timeline in detail, avoids materials → probably underbidding materials
- Doctor mentions three treatment options, only elaborates on one → specialty bias
- Salesperson describes features, avoids limitations → they know the gap

**How to track:** In your notes, keep a "not addressed" column for the meeting.

### 2. Specific numbers vs. round generalities

Real experience produces specific numbers without effort. Improvisation produces round ones.

Compare:
- "About 5 sales a year" (round, generic)
- "7 in 2024, 4 in 2023, all within the Zona Dorada, average 8 months on market" (specific, textured)

The second speaker knows their business. The first is guessing.

**How to track:** Star every specific number in your notes. Count them at the end. Under 5 stars in a 60-min meeting = weak.

### 3. First-hand anecdotes vs. abstract "market" talk

Concrete anecdotes with names, places, and details = experience.
Abstract statements about "the market" or "buyers today" = wallpaper.

Compare:
- "The market is strong right now" (abstract, useless)
- "Last month a Canadian couple paid $X for a similar unit three blocks over, closed in 6 weeks, no contingencies" (concrete, useful)

**How to track:** Count anecdotes with 3+ specific details.

### 4. Reaction to silence

After you deploy silence, watch what fills it:
- **Adds specific data** → confident, expert, worth trusting
- **Adds caveats and softeners** → knew the first answer was inflated
- **Repeats the same claim louder** → improvising
- **Changes topic** → uncomfortable with the answer

**How to track:** Mark each silence deployment with what came after.

### 5. Which option they push

If you presented multiple paths (sell/rent/renovate; surgery/wait/alternative), notice which one they:
- Volunteer numbers for without being asked
- Bring up repeatedly
- Recommend even under hypothetical framing

Cross-reference with their **incentive structure**. If a broker who gets 6% on sale and 8% annually on rent pushes sale, that's aligned with their fast-payout preference — noted but not damning. If they push the option with *less* commission for them, that's high integrity.

**How to track:** Count mentions of each option; note which one they close on.

### 6. Tone shifts

Track moments when the counterparty:
- Speeds up (excitement or nervousness)
- Slows down (measuring words = risk zone)
- Shifts posture
- Breaks eye contact
- Adds unnecessary justifications ("honestly", "to be frank", "I'll be straight with you" — often precede spin)

**How to track:** Note timestamp + what topic triggered the shift.

## The "not addressed" list

Halfway through the meeting, review your notes silently for 30 seconds. Identify topics that were introduced but never resolved. Bring them back at the end with:

> "Earlier you mentioned [X] — we didn't fully unpack that."

This second pass is where the highest-value information often lives. The counterparty relaxes toward the end and answers more honestly.

## Post-meeting scoring grid

Score within 60 minutes of the meeting, while memory is fresh:

| Signal | Score 1–5 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Corrected false statements with data | | |
| Volunteered specific numbers unprompted | | |
| Told first-hand anecdotes with detail | | |
| Filled silence with data (vs. fluff) | | |
| Balanced options / disclosed own bias | | |
| Delivered on promised follow-up in 48h | | |
| Referenced other clients by name / detail | | |
| Reacted openly to competitor mentions | | |

**Interpretation:**
- **32–40**: 🟢 Trustworthy expert. Proceed.
- **20–31**: 🟡 Competent but has bias. Verify with second opinion.
- **Below 20**: 🔴 Pass. Interview another.

## The follow-through test

Set a specific 48h deliverable at the end of every interview (comps, quote, references, whatever fits the domain). Then **do nothing** — no reminders, no nudges.

Who delivers on time and complete = who you can actually work with. Who needs chasing = who will need chasing forever.

This test costs you nothing and reveals more than any question.
